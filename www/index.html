Reduction Stepper

Enter a sequence of SML val declarations and hit Step to watch them reduce one redex at a time. The expression about to be evaluated is highlighted in yellow; anything a substitution just placed is highlighted in green. Click any fn to fold it away — folding is display-only, so the program underneath keeps stepping as it was.

What the language supports
  1. Declarations use val, val rec or fun.
  2. Lambdas must annotate their parameter, and val rec must annotate the whole type — there's no inference engine yet.
  3. Expressions: int, real, string, bool and unit constants, + - ~ * (on ints or reals), div mod (ints), / (reals), ^ (strings), comparisons, andalso, orelse, if…then…else, let…in…end, tuples, case, lambdas, and application.
  4. Patterns: int, string, bool and unit constants, tuples, wildcards and identifiers — in val, in case arms and as lambda parameters. Not reals: SML only matches on an equality type, and real isn't one (nor can you compare two with =).
  5. fun is elaborated into val/val rec, so you get to watch the desugaring. Its first clause needs its argument types annotated (and the result type too, if it's recursive).
